## 3. How we worked

- **Claude's access to the PC:** the chat was linked to this computer. Claude edited files in the connected folders with a shell, used the Blender MCP connection for modelling, and occasionally used screen control.
  - Connected folders: `Projects`, the game folder, and the r2modman folder.
  - The shell runs Linux, so it cannot run the Windows .NET build.
- **Building (you do this):** close R.E.P.O., then double-click `build.bat` in the project folder.
  - The build compiles the mod, installs the DLL into **Test mod**, and makes the Thunderstore zip.
  - If it says "Build FAILED", paste the red lines to Claude.
- **Multiplayer testing:** every player needs the same DLL. Send friends the zip from `dist`, or copy them the DLL.
- **Looking inside the game:** `tools/ildump.py` lists the game's classes and methods and prints their compiled code (IL). It doesn't need any decompiler. Examples:
  - `python3 tools/ildump.py Assembly-CSharp.dll dump ItemGun --il --method "^Shoot$"`
  - `... types "Staff|Orb"`
  - `... refs "batteryBars"`
- **Models:** built in Blender with Python through the Blender MCP. To export, run `tools/export_blender.py` in Blender, for example with `runpy.run_path(...)`.
  - It writes `src/HatterWeaponsPack/Assets/*.mesh`, a custom "HWM1" format that is embedded in the DLL.
  - Axes are converted from Blender (x, y, z) to Unity (−x, z, −y). The muzzle points along Blender −Y, which becomes Unity +Z.
  - Materials named `Glow ...` are rendered self-lit in game.
- **Sounds:** convert with `ffmpeg -i in.mp3 -ac 1 -ar 44100 -f s16le out.pcm` into `Assets/`. The files are embedded automatically, and `SoundLibrary.Get("file.pcm")` loads them.

## 7. Gotchas learned

- Only serialized fields survive when the game clones a prefab. Shared data such as models must live in static fields, as on the builders.
- The game's shader ignores emission, so glowing parts use the unlit `Sprites/Default` shader instead.
- The shotgun is a "one-shot" gun. The wands do their own charge in `ChargedWand`, because the game's built-in charge-up only works for continuous-fire guns.
- The host can't read other players' cameras reliably, so aiming has to be measured on the caster's own game and sent to the host.
- Config files keep their saved values: changing a default in code doesn't change an existing `.cfg`.
- `.cfg` files use CRLF line endings, so edit them with that in mind.
- Copying a game hurt zone also copies its `onImpact*` callbacks. Clear them.
- `Mathf.Pow(Mathf.Cos(x), p)` with a fractional p gives NaN where cos(90°) comes out as −4e-8. One NaN vertex makes the mesh bounds NaN, and Unity silently skips drawing it (the katana wave showed only its light). Clamp with `Mathf.Max(0f, ...)` first.

## 9. HatterMeleeWeaponsPack (new mod, 3 Nov 2026)

Melee weapons, one project with the same layout as HatterWeaponsPack: `build.bat`, csproj, HWM1 models in `Assets`, REPOLib registration. Config: `HatterMeleeWeaponsPack.cfg`.

- **Nunchuck:** brown wood handles (50 cm each, 51 mm across; were 40 cm and 34 mm) and a silver chain (40 cm; was 30 cm).
  - **Model:** `tools/build_nunchuck.py` (run in Blender) builds `blender/nunchuck.blend` and exports `Assets/nunchuck.mesh`. `NC_Handle` lies along +Y from the grip end, with the eye ring's chain point at 0.5232 m (`RING_TOP`, which must equal `NunchuckBuilder.RingTop`). `NC_Link` is one oval link, 4 cm inner length.
  - **Item (`NunchuckBuilder.cs`):** a clone of `Items/Item Melee Baseball Bat`, which keeps its grab, melee grip, swing sounds, shop slot and networking. The bat mesh is hidden. Every `HurtCollider` is neutralised (all logic flags off, zero damage). One grab collider is refitted to the held handle, which starts 8 cm below the bat's `Force Grab Point` and runs towards its `Swing Point`. The price is the bat's × `PriceMultiplier`.
  - **Physics (`Nunchuck.cs`):** built at runtime in a separate scene object, "Nunchuck Chain".
    - (Superseded by the rope redesign below.) Originally: 10 link rigidbodies 4 cm apart, joined by ball joints.
    - Masses are fractions of the bat's: links 0.03 each, free handle 0.35 (were 0.08 and 0.5). The joint to the item has `connectedMassScale` = `ChainPullOnHand` (0.2), so the chain tugs the grip less.
    - Each link has a small sphere collider (0.014 m, ContinuousSpeculative), so the chain rests on floors. The loose parts ignore the item, each other and players.
    - The free handle ignores its own handle's colliders and all player colliders, so it never bumps or launches the holder.
    - The chain hides while the item is in an inventory slot, and is laid out fresh when shown again or if it gets stretched (teleports).
  - **First test (00:53):**
    - Red wireframe: the material had been taken from the bat's first renderer, which is an effect. `PickTemplate` now prefers renderers under "Mesh Healthy", skips wire/outline/unlit shaders, and logs every bat renderer.
    - Stiff chain: links without colliders get Unity's default inertia, which is huge for their mass, so they barely rotated. They now get an explicit rod inertia and low angular damping.
    - Hit speeds seen: 4–26 m/s.
  - **Either end (00:59):**
    - When nobody holds it and the local player holds grab while aiming at the free handle (within `PhysGrabber.grabRange`, nothing closer), the game asks the host to swap, by RPC `HMWPNunchuckSwapRequestRPC` from a non-host.
    - The host reverses its chain (`SwapChain`) and calls `PhysGrabObject.Teleport` to put the item where the free handle was. Teleport is host-only and sends the position to everyone itself.
    - `HMWPNunchuckSwappedRPC(oldPos, oldRot)` then tells the other players to reverse their own chains. The free handle takes the old held-handle pose, and the links reverse order, each flipped 180°.
    - The game's normal grab then picks up the end you aimed at.
    - The builder stores `handleBottom` and `handleRotation` (serialized) for the pose maths.
  - **Second test (01:03):** it got stuck on the floor and the chain clipped through. Four swaps in a row in the log.
    - Causes: too heavy to lift (the dangling parts were about 1.3× the bat, pulling through the joints), links with no collider, and swaps repeating while grab was held (the game grabs on press only, so each retry could swap back).
    - Fixes: lighter parts plus `ChainPullOnHand`, link colliders, swaps on `InputDown` only, and the requester then gets `PhysGrabber.OverrideGrab(item, 0.3, false)` (the swap RPC now carries the requester's actor number). The user's .cfg was updated to the new weights.
  - **Rope redesign (01:10, at the user's request):** the chain links are no longer physics.
    - The free handle is the only extra rigidbody: normal gravity, a capsule collider, ContinuousDynamic.
    - A `ConfigurableJoint` acts as a rope: all linear axes `Limited` to 0.40 m (the chain length) between the two eye rings, angular free, `connectedMassScale` = `ChainPullOnHand`.
    - The ten links are visuals only, drawn in `LateUpdate` along a quadratic curve between the rings. The sag comes from the slack (a parabola of the chain's length), and the chain is straight when taut.
    - `ChainLinkMass` was removed. `SwapChain` now just moves the free handle.
    - The previous versions are kept in the device VM's `/tmp` only (not in the project).
  - **03:24:**
    - Added `MinDamage` 80: weaker hits don't count at all (no damage, push or charge use), and their per-target cooldown is cleared.
    - Charge (durability): the bat's prefab events calling ItemMelee `SwingHit`/`GroundHit`/`EnemySwingHit`/`PlayerSwingHit` are muted with `SetPersistentListenerState(Off)` (`MuteBatDurabilityHooks`; the count is logged). Counted hits call `melee.EnemySwingHit()` (enemies and dummies) or `PlayerSwingHit()` (players) on the host.
    - TargetDummy `NumberLifetime` is now 4.4 s by default (the user's .cfg was updated too).
  - **03:31, after testing (speeds were hard to control, mostly slow):**
    - Damage curve is now logarithmic: damage = MinDamage + (MaxDamage − MinDamage) × ln(v / SpeedForMinDamage) ÷ ln(SpeedForMaxDamage / SpeedForMinDamage). Defaults: 80 @ 8 m/s → 200 @ 30 m/s (117 @ 12, 143 @ 16, 163 @ 20, 180 @ 24).

    - `HitReach` 0.10 m: on the host each physics step, `OverlapCapsule` (handle radius + reach) finds enemies and dummy hitboxes (not walls). Their speed is measured at the handle point nearest the target.
    - Both the contact and reach paths go through `RegisterHit` (shared cooldown, MinDamage gate, charge, log).
  - **Damage (host only):**
    - The free handle's collisions use its contact speed relative to what it hits: damage = `BaseDamage` + (speed − `MinSpeed`) × `DamagePerSpeed`, capped at `MaxDamage`. The defaults are 10, 4 m/s, 7 and 200.
    - Enemies get `EnemyHealth.Hurt`, an impulse of speed × `KnockbackPerSpeed`, and a stun (`EnemyStateStunned.Set`) at `StunSpeed` (16 m/s) or more.
    - Non-enemies get `SendMessageUpwards("TargetDummyHit")`.
    - Players: since the handle passes through them, a capsule check each physics step hurts them with `HurtOther`, never the holder. It skips inactive avatars, i.e. TargetDummy's pseudo-player, whose hitboxes already take the physical hit.
    - There's a per-target cooldown (`HitCooldown`).
    - Every player's game simulates its own chain; hit sounds use the bat's `soundHit` / `soundDink` locally.
